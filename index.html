Same-object safe enhancement for dexterous grasping

SafeAnchor-Grasp

A lightweight second-stage framework that reuses a Raw Grasp prior, expands same-object candidates with AnchorLift, and applies Rescue-Risk Gate for risk-aware replacement in cluttered multi-object scenes.

Manuscript Source Results Data Watch Videos GitHub

Abstract

Risk-controlled same-object enhancement

Dexterous grasping in cluttered multi-object scenes requires more than a geometrically plausible hand pose. The grasp must remain stable during lifting and holding. SafeAnchor-Grasp reuses existing visual grasping priors and performs low-cost same-object second-stage enhancement. AnchorLift binds Raw Grasp to the target object as an anchor seed and generates an AnchorLift candidate from target-local geometry. Rescue-Risk Gate then models Raw Grasp and the AnchorLift candidate as a pair and estimates candidate success, rescue potential, and degradation risk. Across 90 test scenes, integration improves DexDiffuser, GenDexGrasp, and DexGraspNet 2.0 success by approximately 15, 10, and 3 percentage points, respectively. In the DexGraspNet 2.0 analysis, mean success reaches 0.9221, and the observed replacement rate is approximately 12 percent. This rate is not a universal budget for other generators.

Core Message

SafeAnchor-Grasp enhances grasps from different generators while preserving the target object, and jointly evaluates rescue benefit and degradation risk before adopting an optimized candidate.
01

Raw Grasp Anchor

Preserves target-object identity and the useful grasp prior from the original visual grasping system.

02

AnchorLift

Expands the same-object candidate space under anchor constraints instead of acting as a simple local refinement.

03

Structured Physical Supervision

Combines HSF, SSF, and PF to guide same-target contact, hand-structure, and penetration-aware optimization.

04

R2-Gate

Uses a learned intervention policy to compare rescue benefit and degradation risk under the same-target constraint.

Method

Raw Grasp anchor, same-object candidate expansion, and risk-aware replacement

AnchorLift candidate generation

AnchorLift takes target-object point clouds, target-local point clouds, Raw Grasp pose, and hand qpos as inputs. The point cloud feature Fp, local geometry feature FL, pose-anchor feature Fpo, and joint-configuration feature FQ are fused and decoded into an AnchorLift candidate bound to the same target object.

Learned intervention policy

R2-Gate evaluates replacement rather than candidate quality in isolation. It predicts candidate success, rescue potential, and degradation risk from Raw Grasp, AnchorLift candidate, delta features, and object-local cues. The selection rule retains Raw Grasp unless an eligible same-target candidate has sufficient intervention value and meets the degradation-risk constraint. Replacement rates are specific to the generator and evaluation setting.

Robot Experiments

Simulation and real robot execution pipeline

The real platform uses a UR arm, Allegro Hand, and RealSense D455. Ten common everyday objects are absent from the training data. Single-object tests use ten randomized poses per object. Cluttered tests comprise ten rounds of sequentially grasping and removing all ten targets, with new randomized starting poses each round.
